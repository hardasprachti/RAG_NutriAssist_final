- Got: **answered** (decided at: llm; attempts: 1; retrieval: filtered; chunks shown: 5)
- Answer: The Indian dietary guidelines advise using cooking methods that preserve nutrients and avoid nutrient loss, such as preferring pressure or steam cooking over deep frying or roasting, using just enough oil, and avoiding repeated heating of oils.   - The guidelines recommend preferring pressure/steam cooking to deep frying/roasting and using just enough oil, while avoiding repeated heating of oils. _[Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2011; DIETARY GUIDELINES > Adopt right pre-cooking processes and a; `icmr_nin_guidelines_chunk_102`]_
  - Boiling is the most common method of cooking, during which heat‑labile and water‑soluble vitamins like vitamin B‑complex and C are lost; the practice of using excess water while cooking rice should be discouraged and just sufficient water to be fully absorbed should be used. _[Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2011; DIETARY GUIDELINES > Adopt right pre-cooking processes and a; `icmr_nin_guidelines_chunk_101`]_
  - Use of baking soda for hastening cooking of pulses should not be practiced, as it results in loss of vitamins. _[Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2011; DIETARY GUIDELINES > Adopt right pre-cooking processes and a; `icmr_nin_guidelines_chunk_101`]_
  - Microwave cooking is convenient, fast and preserves nutrients, but should be done using glass, pottery, or food‑grade microwave‑friendly containers, avoiding large pieces, mixing food for even heating, and not cooking frozen food directly. _[Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2011; DIETARY GUIDELINES > Adopt right pre-cooking processes and a; `icmr_nin_guidelines_chunk_102`]_
  - Vegetables should be cut into large pieces, washed before cutting, and not soaked in water for long to minimize loss of vitamins due to oxidation and leaching. _[Dietary Guidelines for Indians, 2011; DIETARY GUIDELINES > Adopt right pre-cooking processes and a; `icmr_nin_guidelines_chunk_101`]_
